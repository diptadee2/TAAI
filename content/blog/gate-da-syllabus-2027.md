<div class="eyebrow">Why GATE DA</div>
<h2>Why GATE DA is worth taking</h2>
<p>AI is moving fast, and the research behind it isn't slowing down. GATE DA is one of the cleanest ways into that world, and the score opens up M.Tech, research and PhD routes at the IITs and other strong institutes.</p>
<p>It also sets you up for the right kind of work. Data science and AI is a <b>research field at its core</b>, not just a set of tools you learn to operate. The syllabus covers what sits underneath the libraries, so you can build methods of your own instead of only running what other people built.</p>

<div class="eyebrow">Exam pattern</div>
<h2>GATE DA exam pattern 2027</h2>
<p>If you've looked at any GATE paper before, this one will feel familiar. You get 65 questions for 100 marks in three hours, all on computer. The marks are split between General Aptitude and the core subject, and every question is either an MCQ, an MSQ or NAT type. The <b>one thing that's different</b>, and the only thing worth planning around, is how those marks get spread across the topics. More on that just below.</p>

<div class="eyebrow">Syllabus at a glance</div>
<h2>GATE DA syllabus 2027: the seven core sections</h2>
<p>IIT Madras hasn't changed the DA syllabus for 2027, so this all still holds. Here it is as a quick reference, enough to plan around without reading like a textbook.</p>

<div class="syl">
<div class="cell"><h4><i>01</i>Probability &amp; Statistics</h4><p>Conditional probability &amp; Bayes, random variables, standard distributions, expectation/variance, CLT, estimation, hypothesis testing.</p></div>
<div class="cell"><h4><i>02</i>Linear Algebra</h4><p>Vector spaces, matrices, systems of equations, eigenvalues &amp; eigenvectors, decompositions (LU/QR/SVD), projections, quadratic forms.</p></div>
<div class="cell"><h4><i>03</i>Calculus &amp; Optimization</h4><p>Single-variable functions, limits, continuity, differentiability, Taylor series, maxima and minima, single-variable optimization.</p></div>
<div class="cell"><h4><i>04</i>Programming &amp; DSA</h4><p>Python; stacks, queues, linked lists, trees, hashing; searching &amp; sorting; graph traversals (BFS/DFS); basic complexity.</p></div>
<div class="cell"><h4><i>05</i>DBMS &amp; Warehousing</h4><p>ER &amp; relational models, relational algebra, SQL, integrity constraints, normal forms, indexing, intro to data warehousing.</p></div>
<div class="cell"><h4><i>06</i>Machine Learning</h4><p>Supervised (regression, classification, trees, SVM, kNN, neural nets); unsupervised (k-means, hierarchical, PCA); model evaluation.</p></div>
<div class="cell"><h4><i>07</i>Artificial Intelligence</h4><p>Search (informed, uninformed, adversarial); propositional &amp; predicate logic; reasoning under uncertainty &amp; inference.</p></div>
<div class="cell ga"><h4><i>GA</i>General Aptitude</h4><p>Verbal, quantitative, analytical and spatial aptitude. Worth 15 marks, common to every GATE paper, and the cheapest to secure.</p></div>

<div class="eyebrow">Preparation strategy</div>

<p>The weightage tells you what to study. These four are more about how you study it, so that it stays with you.</p>

<div class="principle">
<div class="pnum">01</div>
<h3>Keep the whole subject in rolling revision</h3>
<p>Don't treat modules as a checklist you finish and forget. As you move forward, keep going back: before Module 2, redo Module 1; before Module 3, redo 1 and 2, and so on. And revision means <b>solving questions without looking at the answers</b>, old tests and quizzes included, not just re-reading.</p>

<h2>GATE DA: frequently asked questions</h2>
<details open><summary>What is the full form of GATE DA?</summary><p>Graduate Aptitude Test in Engineering, Data Science and Artificial Intelligence.</p></details>
<details><summary>How is the paper structured?</summary><p>65 questions, 100 marks, three hours. General Aptitude is worth 15 marks and the seven core subjects carry the other 85. Questions come as MCQs, MSQs and NAT.</p></details>
<details><summary>Which subject has the highest weightage?</summary><p>Going by our read of the 2024 to 2026 papers, Probability &amp; Statistics, at roughly 18 to 21 marks. It isn't officially fixed and it shifts year to year, so use that as a planning guide rather than a rule.</p></details>
<details><summary>Is there negative marking?</summary><p>Only on the MCQs. You lose a third of a mark for a wrong 1-mark MCQ and two-thirds for a wrong 2-mark one. MSQ and NAT questions carry no negative marking.</p></details>
<details><summary>Which programming language is tested?</summary><p>Python.</p></details>
<details><summary>Is the 2027 syllabus the same as 2026?</summary><p>Yes. IIT Madras hasn't revised the DA syllabus for 2027, and the seven core sections have been stable since 2024.</p></details>
<details><summary>Does GATE DA have a separate Engineering Mathematics section?</summary><p>No. The maths you need (probability, statistics, linear algebra and calculus) is built into the core syllabus itself.</p></details>
